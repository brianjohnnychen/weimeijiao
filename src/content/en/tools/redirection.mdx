evidence: emerging

how:
- "Move in early, at the first sign of trouble: a hand reaching for the remote, a tug-of-war starting over a toy."
- "With a baby, simply pick them up or move the object, and offer something else interesting."
- "From toddlerhood, name the limit in a few calm words: “Not the cord. Here’s your ball.”"
- "Offer something that meets the same need: something to bang, throw, climb or squeeze."
- "Make the new option appealing: get down and join in for a minute."
- "If that doesn’t work, change the scene: another room, a look out of the window, a song."
- "Head off repeat battles by moving fragile or dangerous things out of reach."
mistakes:
- "Distracting without ever naming the limit. From toddlerhood, say the rule in a few words too."
- "Reaching for a screen or a snack as the distraction every time."
- "Trying to distract when there is real danger. Stop your child first; see [keeping your child safe](/physical-discipline/#safety-vs-punishment)."
- "Redirecting a child who is hurt or upset instead of comforting them first. See [time-in](/toolbox/#time-in)."
- "Offering a swap that doesn’t meet the need, such as a puzzle for a child who wants to climb."
say:
- "Books are for reading. Let’s find your drum."
- "Gentle hands. You can squeeze this pillow."
- "Balls are for outside. Indoors we can roll this soft one."
- "The table isn’t for climbing. Want to climb the cushion mountain?"
- "Ooh, look what’s outside the window!"

Redirection has little direct research of its own; its case rests on expert guidance and on what is typical at these ages. In its 2018 policy statement, the American Academy of Pediatrics advised against physical punishment and verbal abuse such as shaming, and listed redirecting among the alternatives it recommended, along with positive reinforcement of appropriate behavior, setting limits and setting expectations for the future.<Cite id="aap-2018-discipline" />

Redirection also fits what is typical for toddlers. In a large study, physical aggression was already present at 12 months, rose during the second year of life and declined from the third birthday onward.<Cite id="alink-2006" /> At this age the main job is to keep everyone safe and steer your child toward something better. What is not known is how much of the change over time comes from redirection itself, and how much from simply growing older.
