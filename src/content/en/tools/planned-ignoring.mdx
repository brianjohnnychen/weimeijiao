title: "Planned ignoring"
summary: "Deliberately withdraw your attention from minor, harmless misbehavior such as whining, and give it back warmly the moment the behavior stops."
ages:
- 1-3-years
- 3-5-years
- 5-7-years
- 7-10-years
- 10-12-years
agesNote: "From toddlerhood to about 12, for small attention-seeking behavior such as whining, sulking or silly noises. Not for babies: respond to a baby’s cries. Never for anything dangerous, hurtful or destructive. Preteens: sighs, eye-rolls and muttering are the new whining; ignore the tone and respond to the words."
evidence: moderate
image: tool-planned-ignoring
how:
- "Decide in advance which minor behaviors you will ignore (whining, pestering, sulking, rude noises) and which you will never ignore (hitting, danger, breaking things)."
- "If it helps, say once, calmly, what you are waiting for: “When you use your normal voice, I’ll listen.”"
- "Then withdraw your attention: no eye contact, no talking, no arguing, no sighs. Turn away or busy yourself with something else."
- "Stay close enough to keep a young child safe and to notice when the behavior stops."
- "Be ready: the protest may get louder for a while. Decide beforehand that you will not give in."
- "The moment the behavior stops, turn back warmly and pay attention to what your child is doing now."
- "Pair it with plenty of [specific praise and attention](/toolbox/#specific-praise) at other times."
mistakes:
- "Ignoring something unsafe or hurtful, such as hitting, running off or breaking things. Step in right away."
- "Giving in once the whining gets louder."
- "“Ignoring” with sighs, glares or comments, which is still attention."
- "Ignoring your child as a person, or for a long time. Planned ignoring is aimed at one behavior and lasts only as long as that behavior does."
- "Forgetting to turn back warmly when the behavior stops."
- "Ignoring real distress. A child who is scared, hurt or overwhelmed needs comfort; see [time-in](/toolbox/#time-in)."

- "That’s your normal voice. Now I can help."

Planned ignoring belongs to the core of the <L to="/approach/#ignoring">best-proven approach</L>, and it has been tested on its own. Leijten et al. (2018) pooled 19 experiments that each tested a single parenting behavior, and briefly ignoring a child who had not complied reliably increased compliance, although the estimates of how large the effect is ranged widely.<Cite id="compliance-plos-2018" />

What those experiments tested was short periods of ignoring right after a child refused an instruction. Ignoring whining or sulking applies the same idea; it does not rest on those tests directly. Harmless behavior only: if something is dangerous or someone could get hurt, <L to="/approach/#dangerous-behavior">step in right away</L>.
