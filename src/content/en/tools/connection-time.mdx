title: "Connection time"
summary: "Spend a few minutes of one-on-one time with your child most days, letting them lead the play while you give your full, warm attention."
ages:
- 0-12-months
- 1-3-years
- 3-5-years
- 5-7-years
- 7-10-years
agesNote: "Every age, in a different form. Babies: face-to-face play, copying their sounds and smiles, and responding when they reach for you. Toddlers and preschoolers: 5-10 minutes of play that they choose and lead. Once they start school: a walk, a game or a chat that they pick, with your phone put away."
evidence: moderate
image: tool-connection-time
how:
- "Pick a time you can keep up: 5-10 minutes, most days, one child at a time."
- "Put your phone away and turn off the TV."
- "Let your child choose what to play, and follow their lead. With younger children, blocks, crayons and pretend play are good choices; save games with strict rules or a winner for other times."
- "Describe what your child is doing, like a friendly sports commentator: “You’re putting the red block on top.”"
- "Join in: copy their play, show that you are enjoying it, and praise the things you like."
- "Hold back on questions, instructions, teaching and corrections. This is their time to lead."
- "If your child does something minor you don’t like, look away for a moment and carry on when it stops. If play turns rough or destructive, calmly end it for today and try again tomorrow."
mistakes:
- "Turning it into a lesson: quizzing colors, correcting the drawing, or steering the game toward what you think is better."
- "Taking connection time away as a punishment. Keep it going whatever kind of day it has been."
- "Being there in body only, with one eye on your phone."
- "Giving your full attention mostly when things go wrong, and very little when they go well."
- "Expecting it to replace clear limits. Use it alongside the other tools, not instead of them."
say:
- "It’s our special time. You choose what we play."
- "Wow, you’re building a really tall tower."
- "You gave the dinosaur a blanket. That’s so kind."
- "I love playing with you."
- "Time’s up for today. I’m looking forward to tomorrow."

Programs that teach parents more positive interaction and emotional communication with their child had larger effects in an analysis of 77 parenting program evaluations for children aged 0 to 7.<Cite id="kaminski-2008" /> With babies, the same idea looks like responsiveness: in a randomized trial, a home-visit program that taught mothers responsive behaviors made them more responsive, and that helped their infants’ social, emotional, communication and thinking skills grow.<Cite id="landry-2006" />

What is less clear is how much relationship time adds on top of clear limits and consequences. Two meta-analyses of randomized trials found that programs combining relationship building with behavior management were not better overall than behavior management alone: they did better for children in treatment, worse in prevention programs, and no differently up to 3 years later.<Cite id="leijten-2018" /> So treat connection time as one part of the <L to="/approach/#attention">best-proven approach</L>, not as a fix on its own.
