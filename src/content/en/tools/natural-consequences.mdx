---
title: "Natural consequences"
summary: "When it is safe, let your child experience what naturally follows from a choice, such as feeling cold after refusing a coat, instead of rescuing or lecturing."
ages:
- 3-5-years
- 5-7-years
- 7-10-years
agesNote: "Best suited to ages 5 to 10. With preschoolers, use only small, immediate ones, such as a wobbly tower falling or a game ending when a friend gets upset, and talk about what happened. Not for babies or toddlers: at that age, prevent problems and keep them safe."
evidence: moderate
image: tool-natural-consequences
how:
- "Check three things first: is it safe, is it small, and will it happen soon enough for your child to connect it with the choice?"
- "If any answer is no, step in instead. Never let a natural consequence involve traffic, water, heat, medicines or anything that could cause lasting harm."
- "Give one friendly heads-up, without nagging: “It’s cold out. You might want your coat.”"
- "Then let it happen and don’t rescue: for example, don’t run back to school with the forgotten library book every time."
- "Skip “I told you so.” Show sympathy instead: “Oh no, cold hands. That’s no fun.”"
- "Later, when everyone is calm, help your child think it through: “What could you do next time?”"
- "If the natural result is unsafe, too far off or would mainly hurt someone else, use [logical consequences](/toolbox/#logical-consequences) instead."
mistakes:
- "Letting your child face real danger to “learn a lesson”."
- "Adding a lecture or an “I told you so” on top of the consequence."
- "Choosing consequences that are too far off to connect, such as a school report months away for a 6-year-old."
- "Inventing a punishment and calling it a natural consequence."
- "Letting the consequence land on someone else, such as a sibling whose toy gets broken, instead of stepping in."
- "Expecting a young child to manage alone what they still need help with. Help first with a checklist or with [routines and visual schedules](/toolbox/#routines)."
say:
- "It’s chilly today. Coat or no coat? Your call."
- "Oh no, your drawing got wet. That’s disappointing."
- "The tower fell down. What could you try next time?"
- "You forgot your library book, so no new book today. What would help you remember next week?"

Two meta-analyses of randomized parenting program trials found that natural and logical consequences were among only 3 of 26 techniques linked with stronger effects on disruptive behavior.<Cite id="leijten-2019" /> That analysis grouped natural and logical consequences together, so it does not show how well natural consequences work on their own, and no technique in it, this one included, was linked with stronger effects in the longer term.<Cite id="leijten-2019" />

The everyday rules on this card (safe, small and soon, with sympathy instead of a lecture) are practical guidance rather than tested steps. Natural consequences are never a reason to let a child come to harm: see <L to="/approach/#dangerous-behavior">dangerous behavior</L>.
