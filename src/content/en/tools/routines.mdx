The best direct evidence is for bedtime. In a randomized study of 405 mothers and their babies or toddlers, following a consistent nightly bedtime routine for 2 weeks improved children's sleep: they fell asleep faster and woke up less often and for shorter periods. Mothers' mood improved too.<Cite id="mindell-2009" /> If bedtime is still a battle, behavioral approaches in general have good support: a review of 52 treatment studies found them effective for bedtime problems and night wakings in babies and young children.<Cite id="mindell-2006" />

For routines more broadly, a review of 50 years of research found family routines and rituals related to parents' competence, children's adjustment and marital satisfaction. The studies measured routines in different ways, relied on convenience samples and did not separate direct from indirect effects, so they show links rather than proof that routines cause these benefits.<Cite id="fiese-2002" /> We found no studies of picture schedules for typically developing children at home. The chart is a practical way to make a routine visible, not a tested treatment.
