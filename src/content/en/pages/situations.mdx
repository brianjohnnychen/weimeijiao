<h2 id="intro">How to use these guides</h2>

Find your situation in the list below and read the **Right now** box first: it is written for the moment itself. The rest (why it happens, things to say, prevention and when to get help) is for later, once things have settled.

Every guide builds on <L to="/approach/#core">the best-proven approach</L> and links to the matching cards in the <L to="/toolbox/#intro">Toolbox</L>. The age chips under each title show which ages a guide fits, and <L to="/by-age/#phases">By age</L> has more for each stage.

<h2 id="first-aid">First aid for any hard moment</h2>

1. **Safety first.** If anyone could get hurt, deal with that before anything else: move your child, or hold them calmly (see <L to="/physical-discipline/#safety-holds">safe holds</L>).
2. **Calm yourself, then your child.** Take a breath and lower your voice. In its 2018 policy statement, the American Academy of Pediatrics says that yelling at or shaming children is minimally effective in the short term and not effective in the long term.<Cite id="aap-2018-discipline" />
3. **Use few words.** Say one short, calm sentence, then stop.
4. **Deal with it later.** Save the teaching, talking and problem-solving for when everyone is calm enough to listen.
5. **Respond the same way every time.** In a meta-analysis of 77 evaluations of parent training programs, programs that taught time-out and the importance of consistent parenting had larger effects.<Cite id="kaminski-2008" />

If you lost your temper, you can still make it right: see <L to="/toolbox/#repair">Repair after conflict</L>.

<Note kind="safety" title="If anyone is in danger">

If your child is in danger, or you are afraid you might hurt your child, get help now: see <L to="/about/#help-lines">help lines</L>.
