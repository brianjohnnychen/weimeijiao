<h2 id="intro">How to use the routine chart</h2>

**Make it together.** At a calm time, go through the steps with your child. Cross out any that don't fit and write your family's own steps in the blank rows. Let your child help choose the order and add one step of their own.

**Pictures for children who can't read yet.** Point to each picture as you go. Older children can check their own boxes.

**Notice progress and say it out loud.** When your child does a step without being asked, tell them what you saw: "You got dressed all by yourself!" A check mark or a sticker lets them see how far they have come.

For babies and toddlers, a steady bedtime routine has research behind it. In a 3-week randomized study of 405 mothers and their children aged 7 to 36 months, starting a consistent nightly bedtime routine helped children fall asleep faster and wake less often and for shorter periods at night, and it improved mothers' mood.<Cite id="mindell-2009" />
