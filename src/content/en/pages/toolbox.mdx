<h2 id="intro">Tools that work together</h2>

These tools are meant to be used together, the way the <L to="/approach/#core">best-proven approach</L> combines them: plenty of warm attention and praise for the behavior you want, clear instructions, calm ignoring of minor misbehavior, and brief, consistent consequences when they are needed. Kaminski et al. (2008) compared 77 evaluations of parenting programs for children aged 0 to 7 and found three things linked with larger effects: teaching positive parent-child interaction and emotional communication, teaching time-out together with consistency, and having parents practice new skills with their child during the sessions.<Cite id="kaminski-2008" />

Start with the positive tools at the top of the list: <L to="/toolbox/#connection-time">connection time</L>, <L to="/toolbox/#clear-expectations">clear expectations and instructions</L>, and <L to="/toolbox/#specific-praise">specific praise and attention</L>. Use them every day. Keep the consequence tools, such as <L to="/toolbox/#time-out">time-out</L> and <L to="/toolbox/#privilege-removal">privilege removal</L>, for the times they are not enough.

<h2 id="how-to-use">How to use the cards</h2>

Each card follows the same order: the steps, then the ages the tool fits, common mistakes, words you can say, and links to related tools and situations. Use the age buttons to show only the tools for your child’s age. Practice one or two tools at a time until they feel natural, then add more. If you have used the tools consistently for several weeks and nothing is changing, see <L to="/approach/#not-working">when it is not working</L>.

The badge on each card shows how much research stands behind that tool. Emerging does not mean a tool doesn’t work, only that it has not been studied much on its own. The levels are explained just below.
