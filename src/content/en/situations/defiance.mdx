Refusals wear parents down, but you have more influence than it feels like in the moment. Research points to what you can change: how you give the instruction, and what happens after it.

How you ask: a review of 42 studies concluded that delivering instructions effectively, and starting with a few easy requests your child is likely to follow before a harder one, may be considered evidence-based ways to increase cooperation.<Cite id="radley-dart-2016" /> What happens next: in a meta-analysis of experiments, time-out and briefly ignoring the child after a refusal both reliably increased cooperation.<Cite id="compliance-plos-2018" /> Praise on its own had mixed results for getting children to follow an instruction,<Cite id="owen-2012" /> but it still earns its place: in 2 meta-analyses of parenting program trials, praise and other positive reinforcement, along with natural and logical consequences, were the techniques linked with stronger program effects on difficult behavior.<Cite id="leijten-2019" />

For children 7 and older whose defiance is persistent, <L to="/toolbox/#problem-solving">solving problems together</L> is another option with trial evidence. In a randomized trial with 134 children aged 7 to 14 diagnosed with oppositional defiant disorder, Collaborative and Proactive Solutions, an approach in which adult and child work out solutions together, worked as well as parent management training: after either treatment, about half the children no longer met the diagnosis and were much improved, compared with none on the waiting list.<Cite id="ollendick-2016" />

**At 10 to 12.** Flat refusals turn into negotiation. Arguments get more heated through early adolescence even as they get less frequent,<Cite id="laursen-coy-collins-1998" /> and children who saw no growing say in family decisions turned more sharply toward peers.<Cite id="fuligni-eccles-1993" /> Give a real say in the rules, keep the ones that matter, and take the argument to the family meeting instead of the doorway. See <L to="/by-age/10-12-years/#handing-over">handing over decisions on the Preteens page</L>.
