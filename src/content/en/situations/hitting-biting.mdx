Physical aggression is a normal stage of early childhood, not a sign of a bad child. Among the 2,253 young children Alink et al. (2006) studied, physical aggression already appeared at 12 months, was more common at 24 and 36 months, and declined from the third birthday onward.<Cite id="alink-2006" /> Côté et al. (2006) followed 10,658 Canadian children between ages 2 and 11: most used physical aggression occasionally or rarely as toddlers and rarely or almost never by pre-adolescence, while about 1 in 6 (16.6%) stayed on a high, stable path.<Cite id="cote-2006" />

What helps is a calm response that is the same every time. Kaminski et al. (2008) pooled 77 program evaluations, and the parenting programs that taught time-out and consistent parenting had larger effects.<Cite id="kaminski-2008" /> Two small studies point the same way. Mace et al. (1986) used brief time-out with 3 children, 2 of whom had intellectual disabilities, and aggression and disruption fell.<Cite id="mace-1986" /> In a classroom, Reynolds and Kelley (1997) had each aggressive act cost a smiley face (with a short reprimand), while keeping at least one face earned a reward, and aggression dropped substantially in the 4 preschoolers.<Cite id="reynolds-kelley-1997" /> For the full plan, see <L to="/approach/#aggression">aggression</L> in the best-proven approach.

Hitting or biting a child back to show how it feels can seem logical. But when Gershoff (2002) pooled the studies of parental corporal punishment, it was associated with more immediate compliance and also with more aggression in children,<Cite id="gershoff-2002" /> and, in its 2018 policy statement, the American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that adults not hit children at all.<Cite id="aap-2018-discipline" /> A minority of researchers, Larzelere et al. (2024) among them, argue that the harmful-looking results for ordinary spanking are likely due to other differences between families that the studies could not fully control.<Cite id="larzelere-2024" /> <L to="/physical-discipline/#disagreement">Physical discipline</L> sets out both sides.
